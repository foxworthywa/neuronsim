# Pre-lab: What white blood cells do (BloodSim)

*A 20-minute simulation done at the start of the white blood cell differential lab, before the
smear. There is no separate handout: the lab's own handout (a Google Doc, kept outside this
repository) now opens with "Part 1. Before your smear: BloodSim", a page of boxes that students
fill in scene by scene and keep beside them when they count their own cells. Suggested wording
for Canvas is below.*

---

**Before you make your smear (about 20 minutes).** Open
[BloodSim](https://foxworthywa.github.io/neuronsim/blood.html) on a laptop, with your lab group or
on your own, and work through all five scenes.

1. Guess honestly when the simulation asks you to predict. Predictions are not graded.
2. Fill in Part 1 of your lab handout as you go: one box for each scene. On the last screen,
   type your name (in a group, everyone's names, separated by commas) and write your
   **completion code** on the handout.
3. Your progress is saved in the browser. If the page closes, open it again on the same computer
   and choose **Continue**. On a shared computer, the next group should choose **Start again**.

Your numbers will not match another group's: each group gets a different slide.

### What Part 1 of the handout asks

- **Scenes 1 and 2:** a table with a row for each of the five cells: how I will recognize it
  (nucleus, granules, size), what it does in the tissues, what would raise it in the blood.
- **Scene 3:** lymphocytes before and after the bacterial infection, as a percentage and per µL;
  the two possible explanations for a low percentage.
- **Scene 4:** the group's count of 100 and the lowest and highest counts of the same slide; how
  many counts of the healthy slide had a value outside the reference range, and what that means
  for a value just outside the range on their own count.
- **Scene 5:** the three questions to ask of their own count, copied from the last screen.
- The completion code and names.
