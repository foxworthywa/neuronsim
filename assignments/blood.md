# Pre-lab: What white blood cells do (BloodSim)

*A 20-minute simulation done at the start of the white blood cell differential lab, before the
smear. There is no separate handout: students paste their results and completion code into the
lab handout they already have. Suggested wording for Canvas, and three questions to add to the
handout, are below.*

---

**Before you make your smear (about 20 minutes).** Open
[BloodSim](https://foxworthywa.github.io/neuronsim/blood.html) on a laptop, with your lab group or
on your own, and work through all five scenes.

1. Guess honestly when the simulation asks you to predict. Predictions are not graded.
2. On the last screen, press **Copy our results** and paste them into your lab handout. Type your
   name (in a group, everyone's names, separated by commas) and write down your **completion
   code**.
3. Your progress is saved in the browser. If the page closes, open it again on the same computer
   and choose **Continue**. On a shared computer, the next group should choose **Start again**.

Your numbers will not match another group's: each group gets a different slide.

### Questions to add to the lab handout

These use the simulation to answer the handout's own interpretation questions.

1. *(after the simulation)* Choose two of the five white cells. For each, say what it does in the
   tissues, and name one condition that would raise its number in the blood. Use what you saw in
   scene 2.
2. *(with handout question 2: "Are the values you observed within the normal range?")* In scene 4,
   how many of the counts of the **healthy** slide had at least one value outside the reference
   range? What does that tell you about a value on your own count that is just outside the range?
3. *(with handout question 3: "could they be explained by the questionnaire?")* If one of your
   percentages is low, how could you tell whether you have fewer of that cell, or more of another?
   Use the bacterial infection in scene 3 as your example.
