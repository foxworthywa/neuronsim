### Dana's case

**Dana Brooks, 62,** had a heart attack two weeks ago: a blocked coronary artery killed part of
the muscle of her left ventricle. She gets out of breath climbing one flight of stairs, and sleeps
propped up on three pillows because lying flat makes her breathless. Her doctor hears crackles at
the bottom of both lungs, but her heart sounds are normal, with no murmur. Her echocardiogram
report: *"Left ventricle dilated. End-diastolic volume 180 mL. Ejection fraction 40 %."* She is
started on a diuretic, a drug that makes the kidneys remove extra fluid from the body.

### Section B · How the pump works (15 points)

**1. One loop through the heart (4 points).** Trace a red blood cell from the inferior vena cava
until it leaves in the aorta. Name, in order, every chamber, valve and major vessel it passes, and
at each valve say what pushes it open. Then explain why the pulmonary veins are called *veins*
even though they carry the most oxygen-rich blood in the body.

**2. All four valves shut (4 points).** In every beat there are two brief moments when all four
valves are shut. Using the rule that a valve opens or shuts only because of the pressures on its
two sides, explain why each moment happens. For each one, say whether ventricular pressure is
rising or falling, and what happens to ventricular volume.

**3. Predict: a valve that will not shut (4 points).** A heart attack can also kill a papillary
muscle. Then a bicuspid flap is no longer held by its chordae tendineae, and it blows back into the
left atrium each time the ventricle contracts. Dana's doctor heard no murmur, so this did not
happen to her. Predict, for a patient where it did: (a) where some of the left ventricle's blood
goes during ventricular contraction, (b) what happens to left atrial and lung vein pressure, and
(c) what extra sound a doctor would hear through a stethoscope, and at what point in the cycle.
Explain each prediction.

**4. Correct a classmate (3 points).** A classmate writes: "The heart sounds are the heart muscle
contracting, and the atria pump most of the blood into the ventricles." Correct both claims, using
specific evidence from the traces in scene 4 (the S1/S2 marks, the valve bars and the LV volume
curve).

### Section C · Dana's heart (21 points)

**5. What the echo report means (5 points).** *Test it:* in scene 6, weaken the left ventricle and
compare the dashed loop (before) with the solid loop (after). Report the stroke volume and ejection
fraction you measured for the healthy heart. Calculate Dana's stroke volume from her echo report
(EDV 180 mL, EF 40 %), showing your work. Her stroke volume is close to normal: explain how her
damaged ventricle manages that, and what EF tells a doctor that stroke volume alone does not.
Describe in words how the pressure–volume loop changed after the heart attack.

**6. Why her lungs? (6 points)** Using your scene 6 numbers, explain step by step why damage to
Dana's *left* ventricle puts fluid in her *lungs*. Include: (a) why the two sides of the heart must
pump the same volume per minute; (b) what happened to the two ventricles' outputs in the first few
beats after the damage, and where the difference went; (c) what brought the two outputs back into
balance; (d) why her kidneys' fluid retention raised her blood pressure but made her lungs worse;
(e) why lying flat makes her more breathless.

**7. The diuretic, and the danger of IV fluid (5 points).** Using your diuretic results, state what
happened to her lung vein pressure, stroke volume and blood pressure, and explain the trade-off
using the Frank–Starling law. Then compare two patients who each receive 1 L of IV fluid quickly:
a healthy person who is dehydrated, and Dana. Predict what happens to each, and explain why the
same treatment helps one and could harm the other. Use your IV fluid and fluid retention results.

**8. Transfer: the other side (5 points).** A different patient has severe lung disease that makes
their *right* ventricle fail. Using the idea of two pumps in series, predict where blood would back
up, which pressures would rise, and two signs a doctor might find (think about where the blood
returning to the right heart comes from). Explain why this patient would not get fluid in the lungs
by the same route as Dana.
