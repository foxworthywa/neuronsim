# Lab: The kidney (KidneySim)

*Paste this into a Canvas assignment (a **group** assignment for in-person sections, an individual
one for online sections; submission type **File upload**). The questions are on the handout, which
comes in two versions with the same questions, numbering and points:*

- ***Print version:** `docs/worksheet/KidneySim-handout.pdf` (ten pages, five sheets printed on
  both sides; written and drawn by hand, with every space sized for large handwriting. The table
  of terms is the last page).*
- ***Type-in version:** `docs/worksheet/KidneySim-handout-typed.docx`, made to be uploaded to
  Google Drive and opened as a Google Doc. Before sharing it, put an empty drawing in each of its
  two drawing places (the red notes in the document say how; a drawing cannot be added
  automatically), then give students their own copy: either share a link that ends in `/copy`
  instead of `/edit`, or attach it with Canvas's Google Assignments tool, which makes a copy for
  each student. Replace the bracketed link in "What you need" below with yours.*

*The simulation needs no wet lab and no urinalysis: any urine report in it comes from the model.
The answer key and grading rubric are kept out of this public repository.*

---

**Time:** about an hour in the simulation, plus the handout. In the lab, work in your group: one
person drives the simulation on a laptop or desktop; everyone predicts and discusses. Online
students do the same exercise, alone or with classmates.

**What you need:** the simulation, [KidneySim](https://foxworthywa.github.io/neuronsim/kidney.html),
and the handout in ONE of two versions. They ask the same questions, so pick whichever suits you:

- **Print version** (attached PDF): print it, write and draw by hand, then scan or photograph it.
- **Type-in version** ([make your own copy of the Google Doc](PASTE-YOUR-COPY-LINK-HERE)): type in
  the yellow spaces. For the two drawings, either use the drawing already in the document
  (double-click it) or draw on paper and insert a photo. When you finish, choose File → Download →
  PDF and upload that.

### What to do

1. **Before you open the simulation**, do Part A of the handout (a drawing and one question).
   There are no wrong answers in Part A: it is graded for being done.
2. Work through all ten scenes. At each scene number on the handout, stop and fill in that box.
   **Write your prediction before you press each run button.** The simulation's own prediction
   questions are not graded, so guess honestly.
3. The simulation explains each thing in plain words first and then names it. Each time a yellow
   **names** box appears, copy the term into the table of terms on the handout. The **Terms**
   button at the top of the simulation lists the ones you have reached.
4. You can leave and come back. The numbered circles at the top of the simulation take you
   straight to any scene, so you can redo a scene later to check a number or an idea. Your
   progress is saved in the browser you are using: if you close the page, open it again **on the
   same computer and browser** and choose **Continue**. (On a shared computer, the next group
   should choose **Start again**.)
5. On the last screen, copy **your results** and type **your name** (in a group, everyone's names,
   separated by commas) to get your **completion code**. Paste the results onto the handout (or
   attach them) and write the code in the space after the table of terms.
6. Answer Part C in full sentences, using **your own** numbers from the simulation. Explain *why*,
   not just *what*: the points are for reasoning that links cause to effect.
7. Upload one completed handout as a PDF or as clear photos (one per group, or your own if you
   worked alone).

### Points (50)

| Section | Points |
|---|---|
| A · Before the sim: your drawing; the dialysis question | 4 |
| B · During the sim: scene boxes 1–10, with your predictions and observations, and the table of terms | 16 |
| Results pasted and completion code | 2 |
| C · After the sim: Mr. Herrera; intake changes; urine findings; a drug; failing kidneys; low blood pressure; your redrawn picture | 28 |

### The patients

**Luis Herrera, 56,** has had no working kidneys for two years and has dialysis on Monday,
Wednesday and Friday. The lab opens with his two days between sessions and returns to him at the
end. Scene 8 brings back **Sam Reyes, 19** (type 1 diabetes, a missed insulin dose) and **Dana
Brooks, 62** (fluid retention after a heart attack, starting furosemide). Scenes 9 and 10 add
**Tom Bradley, 41** (heavy bleeding), **Maya Okonkwo, 24** (a half-marathon in the heat) and
**Mrs. Lin, 61** (twenty years of diabetes).
