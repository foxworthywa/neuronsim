# ENDO_MODULE.md — hormones, feedback and blood glucose

**Status:** Part A (seven scenes) is built: `endo/` (engine, app, page), published as `endo.html`;
`tests/endo.test.js` (physiology) and `npm run test:endo` (walks every step in Chromium); the group
worksheet is `docs/worksheet/EndoSim-partA-worksheet.docx` / `.pdf` (generated by
`docs/worksheet/make-endo-worksheet.js`) and the Canvas page is `assignments/endo-part-A.md`. Parts B
and C, the lab and the mystery mode are still design. See "Part A as built" and "Decisions" at the
end.

A third BIO 142 module, built like HeartSim: its own folder (`endo/`), its own engine, the shared
lesson runner in `lib/`, a patient case running through each Part, a results screen with a
completion code, and a worksheet as the graded work. It follows `PEDAGOGY.md` unchanged: predict →
observe → explain, causes before names, nothing drawn that the model did not compute, Continue only
when the question is answered and the activity done.

Endocrinology is hard to run as a wet lab: the signals are invisible and they unfold over hours,
days or years. A model fixes both. It can compress a month into a minute, and it can draw every
hormone at once. The module is built around the one reasoning skill the topic really asks for:
**reading a feedback loop to find where it is broken.**

- **Part A: feedback axes.** The hypothalamus–pituitary–thyroid and –adrenal axes; negative
  feedback, the trophic effect on gland size, rhythms, suppression and stimulation tests; ends in a
  mystery-patient diagnosis with a lab budget (scenes 1–7).
- **Part B: blood glucose.** A compressed 24-hour day of meals, exercise and sleep; insulin and
  glucagon; the student plays the pancreas for a patient with type 1 diabetes; insulin
  resistance and type 2 over years; ends with the oral-vs-IV glucose experiment that reveals the
  gut hormones (scenes 8–13).
- **Part C (later): incretins and the drugs built on them.** GLP-1 and GIP, DPP-4, GLP-1 receptor
  agonists, dual and newer agonists, and how they compare with insulin, sulfonylureas, metformin
  and SGLT2 inhibitors (scenes 14–18). Part B's engine is designed now so Part C is an extension,
  not a rewrite (see "Built for Part C from the start").

## Why feedback before glucose

Glucose control is the endocrine system students care most about, but it is a poor first example
of feedback: two opposing hormones, many target tissues, and meals that disturb it all day. The
thyroid axis is a clean three-level chain with one negative loop. Students learn to read a chain
there (the level *above* a broken gland goes up, the level *below* goes down), then use the same
rule on the adrenal axis, and only then face the pancreas, where C-peptide plays the role that
TSH and ACTH played in Part A.

As in HeartSim, the student **is the controller** first. In scene 2 they set the pituitary's TSH
output by hand to keep thyroid hormone in range as the body's needs change. When the automatic
loop is switched on, it does what they were doing, faster and without overshooting. In scene 10
the controller is taken away again (no beta cells), and the student has to dose insulin.

## Misconceptions each scene targets

Every wrong option gets its own feedback (`PEDAGOGY.md` §5).

| Misconception | Scene | What the model shows instead |
|---|---|---|
| A hormone is sent to its target organ | 1 | It reaches every cell; only cells with its receptor respond |
| Hormones act instantly, like nerves | 2, 5 | T4 takes weeks to settle after a change (half-life ≈ 7 days); a nerve impulse takes ms |
| Negative feedback means "the hormone goes down" | 2, 3 | It means "the change is opposed": TSH rises when T4 falls |
| High TSH means an overactive thyroid | 3 | High TSH usually means the thyroid is *under*-producing and the pituitary is pushing |
| Taking a hormone adds to what the body makes | 3, 4, 6 | The body's own production falls to make room, and the gland shrinks |
| A goiter means too much thyroid hormone | 4 | Iodine deficiency gives a goiter with *low* T4, because TSH drives growth |
| A cortisol level means the same at any time | 5 | Morning and midnight values differ several-fold in a healthy person |
| A steroid course can be stopped any time | 6 | The suppressed axis and shrunken adrenals take weeks to recover |
| Insulin moves glucose into every cell | 8 | Brain uptake does not need insulin; muscle and fat do; the liver's output is switched off |
| Glucagon is only for emergencies | 8, 9 | It holds glucose up every night and between meals |
| Glucose comes only from food | 8, 11 | The liver makes it; with no insulin it makes too much, even while fasting |
| More insulin is always safer for a diabetic | 10 | Insulin plus exercise causes hypoglycemia, which is dangerous within minutes |
| Type 2 diabetes means not enough insulin | 12 | For years insulin is *higher* than normal; it fails to work, then the beta cells fail |
| Normal fasting glucose rules out a problem | 12 | Insulin and the 2-h OGTT are abnormal years before fasting glucose is |
| The pancreas responds only to blood glucose | 13 | The same blood glucose gives more insulin when it arrives through the gut |
| GLP-1 drugs work by making more insulin, so they cause lows | 15 | Their insulin effect depends on glucose; lows come mainly from combining them with insulin or a sulfonylurea |
| GLP-1 drugs cause weight loss by burning fat | 17 | Mostly by lowering hunger and food intake |

## On-stage explanation (the sim explains itself)

- **Narrator bar** generated from model state, e.g. *"Free T4 has fallen to 0.6 ng/dL, below the
  range. The pituitary is no longer being held back, so it releases more TSH (now 14 mIU/L)."*
- **Feedback arrows** drawn on the axis only while they act in the model, with thickness set by
  their strength. A blocked arrow is drawn broken.
- **Lab panel** styled like a real lab report: test, value, units, reference range and an H/L
  flag. It is the same panel the mystery mode uses, so students learn to read real reports.
- **Pattern table** (scene 3) that the student fills in, which becomes a reference card for the
  rest of Part A.
- **Clock and calendar** on every trace, since time scales jump from minutes (insulin) to years
  (type 2). The speed control is labelled in plain words ("1 day per second").
- **Causal checklist** for the meal response: glucose ↑ → beta cells release insulin →
  liver stores glucose, muscle and fat take it up, glucagon falls → glucose returns. Rows light
  while true in the model.
- **Time-in-range meter** (scene 10), the measure continuous glucose monitors report, so the
  score students chase is a real clinical one.

## Free-play lab

Same models, all controls:

- **Thyroid:** iodine intake, gland function (0–150 %), TSH-receptor antibody (Graves'), an
  autonomous nodule, levothyroxine dose, pituitary or hypothalamic failure, cold exposure.
- **Adrenal:** pituitary ACTH tumour (partly resistant to feedback), ectopic ACTH source (ignores
  feedback), adrenal tumour, adrenal destruction (Addison's), prednisone and dexamethasone doses,
  stress level, sampling time of day.
- **Glucose:** meal size and type, exercise, insulin sensitivity, beta-cell mass, rapid and basal
  insulin doses, glucagon, an insulinoma, a sulfonylurea; in Part C the incretin drugs.
- **Views:** any; record any hormone, gland size or glucose trace; export the data table for a
  lab report.

A short GnRH experiment is a candidate lab card: pulsed GnRH drives LH and FSH, but a steady
infusion shuts the axis down by desensitizing the receptors. It explains why leuprolide treats
prostate cancer. It needs a receptor-availability variable on the pituitary, which the generic
axis supports anyway.

## Mystery-patient mode (for assignments)

As in HeartSim: a case code seeds a hidden condition and its severity, so each student's numbers
differ. The student starts with a short history and one basic panel, then **orders tests from a
budget** (each test costs points), records evidence, and submits a diagnosis and its reasoning.
The summary shows the tests chosen as well as the answer, so an efficient diagnosis can be graded
differently from ordering everything. A check code (a hash of the seed and the answers) goes into
Canvas.

Tests available: TSH, free T4, free T3, TSH-receptor antibody, thyroid size (exam), radioactive
iodine uptake; morning cortisol, ACTH, midnight salivary cortisol, low- and high-dose
dexamethasone suppression, ACTH stimulation; fasting glucose, insulin, C-peptide, OGTT, A1c,
urine glucose and ketones.

Initial case pool, chosen so each has a distinct signature in the model:

| Condition | Signature the student can find |
|---|---|
| Primary hypothyroidism (Hashimoto's) | TSH high, free T4 low, gland normal or small |
| Secondary hypothyroidism (pituitary) | TSH low or "normal", free T4 low |
| Iodine deficiency | TSH high, free T4 low, goiter |
| Graves' disease | TSH suppressed, free T4 high, antibody positive, goiter, high iodine uptake |
| Hidden thyroid tablet use | TSH suppressed, free T4 high, small gland, low iodine uptake |
| Cushing's disease (pituitary) | Cortisol high, ACTH high-normal, no low-dose suppression, suppresses on high dose |
| Ectopic ACTH | Cortisol and ACTH very high, no suppression on either dose |
| Adrenal tumour | Cortisol high, ACTH suppressed, no suppression |
| Addison's disease | Cortisol low, ACTH high, no rise with ACTH stimulation |
| Steroid withdrawal | Cortisol low, ACTH low, blunted ACTH stimulation, history of prednisone |
| Type 1 diabetes | Glucose high, insulin and C-peptide low, ketones |
| Type 2 diabetes | Glucose high, insulin and C-peptide high or normal, A1c high |
| Insulinoma | Fasting glucose low, insulin and C-peptide high |
| Hidden insulin injection | Glucose low, insulin high, **C-peptide low** |

The last two rows are the glucose version of Part A's localization rule: injected insulin
carries no C-peptide, so C-peptide tells whether the beta cells made the insulin.

## The models

Two engines in one file (`endo/engine.js`), sharing the units, the browser/CommonJS wrapper, the
event queue (`takeEvents`) and the test harness with `engine.js` and `heart/engine.js`. Both are
semi-quantitative ODE models with invisible equations (`PEDAGOGY.md` §9): the behaviour and the
lab values are right; the equations are never shown.

### Part A: a generic three-level axis

One `Axis` class, instantiated twice (thyroid, adrenal) and once more for the GnRH card:

- **Three hormones:** releasing hormone (H, hypothalamus) → tropic hormone (P, pituitary) →
  target hormone (T, gland). Each is a well-mixed compartment with first-order clearance set by
  its half-life.
- **Secretion:** each level secretes in proportion to its stimulus through a saturating curve,
  times its gland mass, times its own functional capacity (the lesion parameter).
- **Negative feedback:** T inhibits both H and P secretion (Hill-type inhibition); the pituitary
  loop is the stronger. Feedback strength per level is a lesion parameter, so a pituitary tumour
  that only responds to high cortisol is a shift in its inhibition constant.
- **Trophic effect:** gland mass relaxes slowly (weeks) toward a level set by its stimulating
  hormone. This one variable produces goiter (iodine deficiency, Graves'), adrenal atrophy
  (prednisone) and the slow recovery after a steroid taper.
- **Rhythm:** the adrenal axis gets a 24-hour drive on H (peak near waking, low near midnight)
  plus a stress input. Ultradian pulses are off by default.
- **Extras per axis:** thyroid: iodine supply limits T synthesis; peripheral T4 → T3
  conversion; TSH-receptor antibody acts like P but is not under feedback. Adrenal: exogenous
  glucocorticoids (prednisone, dexamethasone) add to the feedback signal, and prednisone also
  counts as cortisol for the body; dexamethasone is **not** measured by the cortisol assay.

Starting half-lives (to be tuned to the targets, not treated as fixed): TRH minutes; TSH ≈ 1 h;
T4 ≈ 7 days; T3 ≈ 1 day; CRH minutes; ACTH ≈ 10 min; cortisol ≈ 60–90 min; gland mass
τ ≈ 3–6 weeks.

### Part B: glucose, insulin and glucagon

Structure based on the published meal-simulation models (Dalla Man, Rizza and Cobelli, 2007, a
basis of the UVA/Padova simulator), simplified, with the Bergman minimal model's insulin action
as the fallback if that proves too heavy:

- **Gut:** stomach → intestine → absorption, with a **gastric-emptying rate** parameter. Meal
  type changes the absorption profile.
- **Glucose:** plasma and tissue compartments. Uptake has an insulin-independent part (brain,
  red cells) and an insulin-dependent part (muscle, fat) scaled by insulin sensitivity. Exercise
  adds uptake that needs no insulin and raises insulin sensitivity for hours afterwards.
- **Liver:** glucose output suppressed by insulin and raised by glucagon; a glycogen store that
  limits how long glucagon can hold glucose up.
- **Kidney:** glucose spills into the urine above a threshold of ≈ 180 mg/dL. The threshold is a
  parameter, which is how an SGLT2 inhibitor will act in Part C and how the kidney module will
  connect.
- **Beta cells:** insulin secretion with a fast first phase and a slower second phase; insulin,
  **C-peptide** and **amylin** released together (equal molar insulin and C-peptide; C-peptide
  cleared more slowly and not by the liver). Exogenous insulin enters a separate depot with
  rapid-acting and basal profiles and has no C-peptide.
- **Alpha cells:** glucagon secretion rises as glucose falls and is suppressed by insulin from
  neighbouring beta cells. In type 1, losing that local insulin is what leaves glucagon
  inappropriately high.
- **Ketones:** a minimal fat → ketone pathway, switched on by low insulin, for scene 11.
- **Long term (scene 12):** beta-cell mass adapts slowly to glucose, as in Topp et al. (2000);
  insulin sensitivity falls along a set trajectory; A1c is computed from mean glucose (the ADAG
  relation, eAG = 28.7 × A1c − 46.7 mg/dL), so A1c lags months behind daily glucose as it does
  in patients.

### Built for Part C from the start

Part C should only add compartments and parameters, so Part B includes these hooks now:

1. **Gut → islet signal:** absorbed nutrient in the intestine drives K-cell (GIP) and L-cell
  (GLP-1) secretion. In Part B the hormones exist in the engine but are not shown; they are why
  scene 13's oral insulin response is larger. Scene 13 therefore reveals a real mechanism rather
  than a scripted bonus.
2. **Glucose-dependent amplification:** incretins multiply the beta cell's glucose-driven
  secretion rather than adding to it, so their effect fades as glucose falls. A sulfonylurea
  adds secretion regardless of glucose. That difference is the whole point of scene 15 and
  must be in the beta-cell equation from day one.
3. **DPP-4 clearance** of GLP-1 and GIP as an explicit rate (native half-life of GLP-1 ≈ 1–2 min),
  so a DPP-4 inhibitor is a change of rate and a GLP-1 agonist is a second, DPP-4-resistant
  ligand with its own half-life.
4. **Receptors as separate targets:** GLP-1R and GIPR on beta cells; GLP-1R effects on alpha
  cells (glucagon ↓), the stomach (emptying ↓) and appetite. A dual agonist is one ligand at two
  receptors. Type 2 blunts the GIP response more than the GLP-1 response, which is why GLP-1
  became the first drug target.
5. **Gastric emptying and food intake as model variables,** with a slow body-weight compartment
  (energy in − energy out), so the weight effect in scene 17 comes out of the model.
6. **Amylin** is already secreted with insulin, so pramlintide and amylin–GLP-1 combinations
  can be added without new beta-cell code.

### Engine API (sketch)

```js
const thy = new Axis('thyroid');        // healthy adult; also new Axis('adrenal'), new Axis('gnrh')
thy.advance(dt);                        // dt in minutes; internal steps adapt to the fastest half-life
thy.levels                              // {TRH, TSH, T4, T3}  thy.mass // {pituitary, thyroid}
thy.setLesion('primary', 0.3);          // also 'secondary', 'iodine', 'gravesAb', 'nodule', …
thy.setDrug('levothyroxine', µgPerDay);
thy.labPanel(['TSH', 'freeT4']);        // values with units, ranges and H/L flags

const g = new Glucose();                // healthy adult, fasting, 7 am
g.advance(dt);                          // minutes
g.eat({ carbs: 60, kind: 'mixed' }); g.exercise({ minutes: 30, intensity: 0.6 });
g.inject('rapid', units); g.inject('basal', units); g.inject('glucagon', mg);
g.setBetaMass(0); g.setSensitivity(0.4);
g.state     // {glucose, insulin, cPeptide, glucagon, ketones, urineGlucose, gip, glp1, …}
g.takeEvents();                         // [{type:'hypo', level:54, t}, {type:'meal', t}, …]
```

Time is in minutes throughout this module (not ms): the fastest process that matters is insulin
clearance.

### Speed

Part A runs at 1 day per second for the loop scenes and up to 1 month per second for the trophic
and taper scenes. Part B's day runs at about 1 hour per 3 seconds, scene 12 at 1 year per
press. Both models are cheap, so speed is a teaching choice, not a computing limit.

## Acceptance tests (`tests/endo.test.js`)

- Each axis settles to the targets from any reasonable starting state, and stays there for a
  simulated year.
- Localization rule: for each lesion level in each axis, the level above moves opposite to the
  level below, as in the scene 3 table.
- Levothyroxine in a healthy person: TSH falls, the body's own T4 output falls, total T4 rises
  much less than the dose alone would predict, thyroid mass falls.
- Iodine deficiency: free T4 low, TSH high, thyroid mass up ≥ 50 % within 6 months.
- Graves': TSH suppressed below 0.1 while T4 stays high (the antibody is not under feedback).
- Cortisol peaks within 2 h of waking and is lowest near midnight.
- Prednisone for 8 weeks: ACTH and adrenal mass fall; abrupt stop gives cortisol below 3 µg/dL
  for days; a 4-week taper avoids it.
- Dexamethasone: suppresses healthy cortisol, partially suppresses Cushing's disease only at high
  dose, never suppresses ectopic ACTH or an adrenal tumour.
- Glucose stays 70–99 mg/dL through a 16-h fast without intervention; brain uptake is unchanged
  when insulin is set to zero.
- A 75 g oral load peaks below 160 and returns below 140 by 2 h in health.
- Type 1 with no insulin: glucose rises while fasting; ketones rise; urine glucose appears only
  above the renal threshold.
- Rapid insulin + exercise without extra carbohydrate gives glucose below 70 mg/dL.
- Glucagon raises glucose only while liver glycogen remains.
- Over the scene 12 trajectory, fasting insulin rises years before fasting glucose crosses 100.
- Injected insulin raises insulin without raising C-peptide; an insulinoma raises both.
- Oral vs matched IV glucose: oral insulin response ≥ 1.5× IV in health; the ratio is lower in
  type 2. (Part C hook: the response disappears if GLP-1 and GIP are set to zero.)
- Glucose-dependence: a fixed GLP-1 level raises insulin at 180 mg/dL and has little effect below
  ≈ 70; a sulfonylurea raises insulin at both.

- Same background, panels and trace style as the other modules.
- One fixed colour per hormone family, never reused for an ion. Glucose, insulin, glucagon,
  C-peptide, GLP-1 and GIP need colours distinct from Na⁺/K⁺/Cl⁻/Ca²⁺ (to be chosen and listed
  here); the thyroid and adrenal hormones can share a second set because they never appear
  together.
- Gland size on screen is the model's gland mass. Particles in the blood are drawn at a density
  set by the concentration, scaled per hormone (labelled, since concentrations differ by 10⁶).
- Reference bands drawn behind every hormone trace, so "high" and "low" are visible without
  reading numbers.

## Accessibility (design in, not retrofit)

As HeartSim: keyboard-operable controls, an `aria-live` narrator, nothing shown by colour alone
(lab flags say H or L in text), a reduced-motion setting.

## Out of scope for this module

Molecular detail of receptors and second messengers (a possible short add-on for Part A), the
posterior pituitary and ADH (the kidney module), calcium and PTH (a candidate lab card later),
growth hormone, the reproductive cycle (beyond the GnRH card), DKA treatment and electrolytes,
insulin pump algorithms, scoring and accounts.

## Part A as built (differences from the plan above)

- **Two classes, not one `Axis` class.** `Thyroid` and `Adrenal` share the pattern (and the
  helpers) but differ enough in their extras that one generic class would have been harder to
  read. TRH and CRH last minutes, so they are computed from the feedback signal directly rather
  than given their own compartments.
- **The pituitary's cells adapt.** Weeks of strong stimulation build up the TSH-making cells, and
  weeks of suppression shrink them (`capP`, τ = 30 days). This is what makes TSH lag weeks behind
  free T4 after a dose change (Maria at week 1: free T4 already 1.26 ng/dL, TSH still 6.2 mIU/L;
  week 6: 2.0), and it is the real reason for the six-week recheck. In the adrenal axis, the CRH
  and ACTH cells lose capacity under a strong glucocorticoid and recover over weeks (τ = 7 days),
  which, with the adrenal's own shrinkage (τ = 14 days), produces Daniel's crisis and the benefit
  of a taper.
- **Prednisone is a daily dose** at 8 am, with a biological half-life of about 12 h; its effect is
  expressed as cortisol-equivalent µg/dL (3 per mg). Dexamethasone is 75 per mg, with a 36-hour
  half-life, and is not seen by the cortisol test.
- **Scene 1 is a cell view**, not the axis: a TSH pulse reaches four kinds of cell, only the
  thyroid cell (with TSH receptors) responds, and then T4 is shown entering every cell and binding
  receptors in the nucleus.
- **Scene 6 traces show daily averages,** because over 14 weeks the daily rhythm and the daily
  dose would otherwise fill the plot with sawteeth.
- **No mystery mode yet:** scene 7's two patients are worked through with the student choosing
  the interpretation, not the tests. The budgeted mystery mode is M2.
- Measured with the default model: healthy TSH 1.5 mIU/L, free T4 1.30 ng/dL, free T3 3.0 pg/mL,
  thyroid 18 g; Maria (gland function 16 %) TSH 58, free T4 0.74; iodine-poor diet for 6 months:
  thyroid 72 g, TSH 64, free T4 0.71; T4 tablets 150 µg for 6 months: thyroid 5 g, own output
  89 → 3 µg/day. Cortisol 15 µg/dL at 8 am and 2.5 at midnight (ACTH 31 and 3); 6 h of stress:
  17 µg/dL at 6 pm against 7.4. Prednisone 20 mg for 8 weeks: adrenal 4.3 → 1.1 g; stopped
  abruptly, the lowest daily cortisol effect is 1.1 µg/dL with about a week below 3 (crisis);
  tapered over 7 weeks, the lowest is 4.7 with none. Dexamethasone 1 mg: 8 am cortisol 0.2;
  cosyntropin: 16 → 32 µg/dL at 60 min; adrenal tumour after dexamethasone: 32; Addison's after
  cosyntropin: 2.3 → 3.5.
