A fifth BIO 142 module, built like ImmuneSim: its own folder (`kidney/`), its own engine, the
shared lesson runner in `lib/`, a patient running through it, a results screen with a completion
code, and a handout as the graded work. It follows `PEDAGOGY.md` unchanged: predict → observe →
explain, causes before names, nothing drawn that the model did not compute, Continue only when the
question is answered and the activity done.

**Length: about 55 minutes, one Part, ten scenes.** It stands alone: it assumes no wet lab and no
urinalysis, and works for an online student working alone as well as for a group in the lab.

## What students lack now

The chapter is organised by anatomy: the forces at the glomerulus, then each stretch of the tubule
with its list of what moves in which direction. Students memorise the lists and leave without the
two things the lists depend on (instructor, Nov 2026):

- **what the kidneys are for**, and why the body makes urine at all;
- **that the kidney filters the blood and then takes back what must be kept.**

Without those, the detail has nothing to attach to, and students cannot use any of it on a
problem. The module teaches those two things thoroughly and leaves the lists to the textbook.

## The mental model the module builds

Four claims. Each is discovered by experiment and then used again.

1. **The kidney is the body's adjustable exit.** What enters the blood each day (water, salt,
   potassium, the wastes of metabolism, drugs) varies widely and has nothing to do with what the
   body needs. Cells tolerate almost no change in the fluid around them. Lungs, skin and gut lose
   things at rates the body does not set for this purpose. The kidney is the one exit that can be
   adjusted, so it makes up the difference exactly. **Urine is the remainder:** it varies so that
   the blood does not.
2. **Filter everything small, then take back what you want.** The list of things to remove is
   open-ended and includes substances the body has never met. The list of things to keep is short
   and known. So the kidney pushes everything small out of the blood and takes the short list
   back. The cost is filtering a huge volume and taking nearly all of it back, which is why 180
   litres are filtered a day to make one or two litres of urine.
3. **The filter is not selective; the tubule is.** The filter sorts by size and nothing else. It
   does not know what is waste. Every choice the kidney makes is made afterwards, along the tubule.
4. **Control is in how much is taken back.** Most of the filtrate is taken back early and
   automatically. The last few percent is adjustable, substance by substance, and that is how the
   exit is matched to the day's intake. Hormones carry the instruction.

### One picture and three questions

The picture: blood → filter → tubule → urine, with a **return line** running from the tubule back
to the blood. It is on screen from scene 4 to the end.

For any substance, the student asks:

1. Did it get through the filter?
2. Was it taken back?
3. Was any extra pushed in?

What is left is in the urine. Written as a sum: **excreted = filtered − reabsorbed + secreted.**

### What a student should be able to do afterwards

The sim and the handout are designed backward from these five problems. Part C of the handout is
these problems, set on the patients.

1. **Intake changes.** Given a litre of water, a salty meal, a day of sweating or a bleed, predict
   which way the volume and the concentration of the urine go, and say why.
2. **Urine findings.** Given protein, glucose, or a large dilute volume in the urine, say which
   step failed.
3. **Drugs.** Given a drug that blocks the return of something, predict the effect on the urine
   and on the blood.
4. **Failing kidneys.** Say what accumulates in the blood, and why.
5. **Low blood pressure.** Explain why it threatens the kidney.

## Why a model

- **Scale.** A figure cannot show that 125 mL a minute enters the tubule and 1 mL a minute leaves
  it. The model draws the stream at its real width.
- **Time.** Balance is a matter of hours and days. The model runs a day in a minute.
- **The purpose can be felt.** The student does the kidney's job by hand before seeing how the
  kidney does it, as in HeartSim (squeeze the ventricle) and EndoSim (be the pituitary).
- **Taking a step away.** Damaging the filter, saturating a carrier or blocking a return cannot be
  done on the bench. In the model each is one switch, and the urine changes as it would in a
  patient.

**The student is the controller first:**

- Scene 2: the student **is the exit**, setting by hand how much water, salt and potassium leave
  each hour while a healthy person eats, drinks and sweats through a day.
- Scene 4: the student **is the designer**, and finds that a machine with one pump for each waste
  fails the first time a new substance arrives.
- Scene 9: the student **is the last stretch of the tubule**, and then hands the job to the
  hormones, which do what the student was doing.

**Three things stay on screen**, so that scenes add to one picture:

- **The blood panel.** Body water (as weight, the way a dialysis nurse reads it), potassium, urea
  and, from scene 9, sodium, each with its reference band. It is laid out like a lab report, as in
  EndoSim.
- **The nephron strip** (from scene 4). The filter on the left, the tubule drawn straightened, the
  urine on the right, and the return line beside the tubule. The width of the stream is the flow.
  Each substance is a particle of its own shape and colour, drawn in proportion to what the model
  holds.
- **The day graph.** Two traces on one time axis: a blood value, nearly flat, and the urine, which
  swings. It is the picture of claim 1, and it plays the part the infection timeline plays in
  ImmuneSim.

A **ledger** (filtered, taken back, pushed in, in the urine; amounts per day) sits under the strip
from scene 6 and fills in one substance at a time.

## The through-case

**Luis Herrera, 56,** has had no working kidneys for two years and has hemodialysis on Monday,
Wednesday and Friday. The lab opens with his two days between sessions (scene 1), builds the
healthy kidney he lacks (scenes 2 to 9), and returns to him at the end (scene 10), where the
student can say what his machine does, why he is weighed before each session, and why his diet
restricts fluid and potassium. A person with kidneys eats and drinks freely and the exit adjusts.
A person without them has to adjust the intake instead.

The healthy person of scenes 2 and 9 is unnamed ("a nursing student on an ordinary day"), so that
the student's attention stays on the contrast with Mr. Herrera.

Short cases in scenes 8 and 10 bring back patients from the other sims, where something about the
kidney was stated and not explained:

- **Dana Brooks** (HeartSim): fluid retention after her heart attack, then a diuretic.
- **Sam Reyes** (EndoSim Part B): type 1 diabetes, glucose in the urine above about 180 mg/dL,
  thirst and a large urine volume.
- Two new one-paragraph patients: a runner after two hours in the heat, and a man in the
  emergency department after a bleed, with a blood pressure of 78/50 and almost no urine.

## The model

One engine (`kidney/engine.js`), sharing the browser/CommonJS wrapper, the event queue
(`takeEvents`) and the test harness with the other engines. Semi-quantitative, equations invisible
(`PEDAGOGY.md` §9). **Time is in minutes.** Runs are exactly repeatable.

### The body

- **Compartments:** plasma (about 3 L) inside extracellular fluid (about 14 L) inside total body
  water (about 42 L). Water moves between cells and extracellular fluid to keep their
  concentrations equal.
- **Tracked substances:** water, sodium, potassium, urea, creatinine, glucose, a blood protein
  (albumin) and one drug. Potassium is mostly inside cells; cells take up part of a meal's
  potassium within the hour and give it back slowly.
- **The way in:** a script of meals and drinks (each a list of amounts), and steady production of
  urea and creatinine.
- **The other ways out:** lungs and skin lose water at a rate set by activity and heat, sweat
  carries some salt, the gut loses a little potassium. None responds to the blood panel.
- **Blood pressure** follows blood volume through a simple curve. HeartSim's engine is not
  imported.

### The nephron (one, standing for all)

- **Filter.** Filtering rate = a constant × the pressure across the filter. Each substance has a
  passing fraction set by its size: 1 for water, salts, glucose, urea, creatinine and the drug;
  about 0 for albumin; 0 for cells. The arteriole feeding the filter adjusts to hold the pressure
  across it steady while arterial pressure is between about 80 and 180 mmHg, and cannot below that.
- **First stretch (automatic).** Takes back a fixed share of the salt, with water following in
  proportion. Glucose is taken back by a carrier with a maximum rate, so it appears in the urine
  only when the filtered amount exceeds what the carrier can move. About half the urea drifts
  back with the water. Creatinine is not taken back. The drug is pushed in by its own carrier.
- **The loop** takes back salt without all of its water and keeps a salty inner kidney. In Part A
  this is one state variable (how concentrated the urine can be made, up to about 1,200 mOsm/L)
  that nothing on screen refers to. It is here so that a salt-carrier blocker gives the right
  result now and so that Part B is an extension, not a rewrite.
- **Last stretch (adjustable).** Salt taken back follows aldosterone. Water taken back follows
  ADH, up to the limit the loop allows. Potassium is pushed in at a rate set by aldosterone and by
  plasma potassium.
- **Water follows solute.** Whatever solute stays in the tubule holds water with it, because the
  urine cannot be more concentrated than the limit. Glucose left behind by a saturated carrier and
  salt left behind by a diuretic both raise the urine volume by this one rule.

### Control

- **ADH** rises with the concentration of the blood, and with a large fall in blood volume.
- **Aldosterone** rises as blood volume falls (the steps between are not modelled in Part A) and
  as plasma potassium rises.
- In scenes 2 and 9 the student's dials replace these two signals.

### Faults, drugs and the machine

Each is one parameter: the filter's passing fraction for albumin and cells (damage); the glucose
carrier's maximum (the drug that blocks it); the loop's salt uptake (Dana's diuretic); the number
of working nephrons (from 100 % to 0); ADH on or off (kept for Part B). **Dialysis** is a second
filter outside the body with its own clearances for urea, creatinine and potassium and a set
amount of water to remove, run for four hours.

### Engine API (sketch)

```js
const body = new Kidney();                      // healthy adult; body.set('nephrons', 0) for Mr. Herrera
body.take({ water: 1000 });                     // mL; also salt (g), potassium (mmol), glucose (g), drug (mg)
body.lose({ sweat: 1100 }); body.bleed(800);    // mL
body.advance(dt);                               // dt in minutes
body.blood      // {water, weight, sodium, potassium, urea, creatinine, glucose, osmolarity, pressure}
body.nephron    // {gfr, flow: [filter, endFirst, endLoop, endLast], urine: {flow, osmolarity, colour}}
body.ledger('glucose')   // {filtered, reabsorbed, secreted, excreted} per day
body.hormones   // {adh, aldosterone}
body.manual({ water: 0.6, salt: 0.4, potassium: 0.5 });  body.auto();
body.set('filterDamage', 0.5); body.set('glucoseCarrier', 0.4); body.set('loopSalt', 0.3);
body.dialyse({ hours: 4, removeWater: 2500 });
body.urineReport();   // protein, glucose, blood, volume per day, colour, with flags
body.labPanel(['Na', 'K', 'BUN', 'creatinine', 'glucose']);
body.takeEvents();    // [{type:'outOfRange', what:'potassium', t}, {type:'glucoseInUrine', t}, …]
```

### Speed

About one simulated hour per second for the day runs (scenes 1, 2, 9, 10), with a visible clock.
The strip scenes (4 to 8) run at a steady state and show flow, so they have no clock.

## Physiological targets (become `BIOLOGY.md` § Kidney and the tests)

Starting values, written from memory of the textbook ranges; to be tuned and then checked against
the edition of McKinley in use.

| Quantity | Target |
|---|---|
| Blood flow to the kidneys | about 1.1 L/min, 20–25 % of cardiac output |
| Filtering rate (GFR) | about 125 mL/min, 180 L/day; plasma volume filtered about 60 times a day |
| Share of the plasma passing the filter that is filtered | about 20 % |
| Urine volume | 1–2 L/day on an ordinary day; from about 0.5 L/day to well over 10 L/day |
| Water taken back | about 99 % of what is filtered |
| Share taken back in the first stretch | about two-thirds of the water and salt |
| Urine concentration | about 50 to 1,200 mOsm/L |
| Plasma concentration | 285–295 mOsm/L, held within the band through the scene 2 day |
| Sodium | 135–145 mmol/L; about 25,000 mmol filtered a day, more than 99 % taken back |
| Potassium | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L; about 60 mmol in all the extracellular fluid; 50–100 mmol eaten a day |
| Glucose | about 180 g filtered a day, none in the urine below about 180 mg/dL |
| Urea | BUN 7–20 mg/dL; about half of what is filtered is excreted |
| Creatinine | 0.6–1.2 mg/dL; excreted ≈ filtered |
| Albumin | essentially none in the filtrate |
| Filtering against pressure | within 10 % of normal for a mean arterial pressure of 80–180 mmHg; falling steeply below; near zero at about 50 |
| A litre of water | urine flow peaks in about an hour and most of the litre is out within three |
| No kidneys, ordinary diet | weight + 1 to 1.5 kg a day; BUN + 20 to 25 mg/dL a day; potassium + 0.5 to 1 mmol/L over two days |
| Dialysis session | about four hours; urea falls by about two-thirds; weight back to its target |

## Acceptance tests (`tests/kidney.test.js`)

- Water, sodium, potassium, urea and creatinine are each conserved: intake + production = change
  in the body + all losses, to within 0.1 % over a day.
- Resting targets in the table above are met.
- A healthy body run through the scene 2 day on Automatic keeps every blood panel value in range,
  while urine flow varies at least tenfold and urine concentration at least fivefold.
- With no nephrons, the same day takes weight, potassium and urea out of range, and no setting of
  sweating or breathing brings them back.
- With the return switched off, plasma volume is gone in 20–30 minutes.
- Albumin: not filtered. Glucose at 90 mg/dL: filtered, none excreted. Creatinine: excreted =
  filtered within 5 %. The drug: excreted > filtered.
- A substance with no carrier of its own is cleared from the blood with a half-life of hours.
- Glucose appears in the urine only above the threshold, and the amount excreted then rises in
  step with blood glucose; urine volume rises with it.
- Blocking the glucose carrier gives glucose in the urine at a normal blood glucose.
- Filter damage gives albumin and red cells in the urine and leaves the glucose result unchanged.
- Blocking the loop's salt uptake raises the salt and the water excreted, and lowers the most
  concentrated urine the kidney can make.
- A litre of water: urine flow rises at least fivefold and urine concentration falls below
  plasma's; filtering rate changes by less than 5 %.
- A salty meal: sodium excretion rises over the next hours; filtering rate changes by less than 5 %.
- Two hours of sweating: urine flow falls below half and concentration rises above 800 mOsm/L.
- A bleed that takes mean pressure to 60 mmHg: filtering rate below 30 % of normal, urine flow
  near zero.
- Taking back 1 % less of the filtered water adds about 1.8 L of urine a day.
- Half the nephrons, at steady state: creatinine within 10 % of double; a quarter: about fourfold.
- A dialysis session returns weight to its target and potassium and urea to their post-session
  values, and they rise again at the scene 1 rates.

## Visual style (extends `VISUAL_STYLE.md`)

- Same background, panels and trace style as the other modules.
- One fixed colour and shape per substance, the same on the strip, the ledger and the graphs, and
  not one already used for an ion in NeuronSim or a hormone in EndoSim (to be chosen and listed
  here). Sodium and potassium keep their NeuronSim colours.
- Blood is red on both sides of the kidney, never blue (as HeartSim).
- Urine colour is computed from its concentration, pale to amber.
- The real nephron and the straightened strip are shown morphing one into the other in scene 4,
  and the strip keeps a small real-nephron inset afterwards, as HeartSim keeps the real heart.

## Accessibility (design in, not retrofit)

## Part B (later): water and salt as two separate problems

Decided as second tier (instructor, Nov 2026). The engine carries what it needs from the start.

- **How urine becomes more concentrated than blood.** Water cannot be pumped, so taking back water
  without salt needs a salty place for it to go. The loop builds that place; ADH decides whether
  the last stretch uses it. The student switches off the loop's salt uptake and watches ADH stop
  working. The stepwise multiplier is not taught.
- **Volume and concentration.** Water sets the concentration of the blood (ADH, thirst). Sodium
  sets its volume (aldosterone, renin and angiotensin). A low sodium value is usually too much
  water. A 2 × 2 table of water and salt, lost and gained.
- **Patients:** diabetes insipidus, too much ADH, Dana Brooks's heart failure in full (the link to
  HeartSim's blood pressure), a patient on an ACE inhibitor.

Acid–base is a separate module, with the lungs.

- **Model details settled in the build.** Potassium: a share of any excess stays outside the
  cells (11 % in health, 20 % for Mr. Herrera), and cells take up the rest within about 15
  minutes; a failing kidney's surviving nephrons each push out more, so potassium stays in range
  until filtering is below a quarter. Glucose carriers: a maximum of 375 mg/min, filling
  unevenly, so that glucose first escapes at about 180 mg/dL and every carrier is full by about
  300. For runs of days the body can be given thirst, so that a kidney which cannot concentrate
  its urine does not dry the body out. The
  last stretch can take back at most 2.1 mmol of sodium a minute, which is what lets a loop
  diuretic overwhelm it. Urine concentration runs from 50 mOsm/L to the limit the loop allows
  (1,200), and a flood of solute lowers that limit. A diuretic tablet is absorbed over half an
  hour and acts for about six. Blood pressure follows blood volume; after a bleed, fluid from
  the tissues refills the plasma over hours.

  82.0 → 84.4 kg, potassium 4.0 → 5.4, BUN 25 → 69; after dialysis 81.9 kg, 3.8, 25. Blood

  blocked: 81 g a day. Damaged filter: 5.8 g of protein a day. A 1.5 L bleed, half an hour on:
  80/52, filtering 34 mL/min, urine 11 mL an hour. A quarter of the filtering: creatinine 3.6,
  potassium 4.8, BUN 50, urine 1.7 L a day.
